---
title: Against Consensus
date: 2026-06-10
description: On why the most valuable ideas look wrong before they look obvious.
---

Every important decision I've made looked like a mistake from the outside.

Leaving the degree with a year left. Turning down the safe seat for the one nobody had heard of. Spending a season building a thing that "wouldn't work." Each time, people I respected told me to wait, to be reasonable, to take the obvious path. Each time I asked them why — not to be difficult, but because I genuinely wanted to be wrong. I'd ask why until I either understood, or until the answer ran out.

The answer usually ran out.

Consensus is a kind of compression. It takes everything a crowd believes and flattens it into one safe shape. That shape is almost always right about the past and almost always wrong about the future, because the future is exactly the part the crowd hasn't priced in yet. If everyone can already see it, the opportunity is gone.

So I've learned to treat unanimous agreement as a warning, not a comfort. When a plan makes everyone nod, I get nervous. When it makes the sharpest person in the room uncomfortable but unable to say exactly why — that's usually where the value is hiding.

You don't get to be right and look right at the same time. Pick one.
